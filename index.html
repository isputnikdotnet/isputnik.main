isputnik

Your family's media library, under your own roof. Audiobooks, ebooks, photos, and videos — served from your home, for the people you trust.

Inside isputnik.home

One server, the whole shelf.

Everything a household keeps — the books it listens to, the books it reads, the photographs it can't throw away, the people in them — in one place that belongs to you. No subscription. No cloud. Nothing leaves the building unless you send it.

Audiobooks

Every chapter. Every narrator. Your place, kept.

A folder of files becomes a book with chapters, a cover, the reader who recorded it, and its place in the series. Stop on your phone, pick up on the laptop, come back a year later — the bookmark is still there.

  • Chapters, bookmarks, playback speed, and a sleep timer
  • Series in order, narrators as first-class people you can browse
  • Download to the phone and keep listening where the server can't follow

Ebooks

Read it here. Or send it to the Kindle.

EPUB and FB2 open in a reader built in; PDF opens as itself. The same title in three formats is one book, not three. A second translation, another publisher's text, the audiobook of a book you also own — those are editions, grouped on one page with the one you're reading marked.

  • Highlight a passage and keep it as a quote — it remembers the page
  • OPDS feeds, so your own e-reader app can browse the shelf
  • Send a book straight to a Kindle or Kobo from its page

Photos & videos

Every photo, sorted by itself.

Point it at the folders you already have. Photos land on a timeline by the day they were taken, the same day in earlier years surfaces on its own, and nothing is copied or moved — your files stay exactly where they were.

  • Timeline, folders, albums, slideshows, people and a map — all views of one library
  • "Around this day" on the home page, every morning
  • Fix a wrong date or a missing place for a hundred photos at once

Face recognition

Faces become people. On your machine.

Turn it on for a library and a background job finds faces and gathers them into people. Name the ones who matter and the name sticks. The models ship with the app; not one pixel is sent anywhere to do it.

  • Merge two groups that are one person, or move a stranger out of a group
  • A person's photos become a filter, an album, a slideshow suggestion
  • Entirely local, entirely optional, off by default

Map

Where it happened.

Anything with a location joins the map, gathered into clusters until you zoom close enough to tell them apart. Photos without one can be placed by hand — search for the town, drop a pin, done for the whole batch.

Stories

The trip, told properly.

A story is a page you write yourself — your words, with the photos, the album, the map of the route, a person from the family tree, a quote, the book you were reading — placed where they belong in the telling. One scrollable page, or a chapter per day.

  • Points at what's already in the house; never copies it
  • A map block with stops draws the route between them — on foot, by car, by plane
  • Send it to family, or share a link with anyone

Family tree

Who they were to each other.

Parents above, children below, partners alongside. Every person has a profile — their timeline, their photos, the things they said — and the people found in the gallery can be linked straight to their place on the tree.

  • Second marriages, adoptions and half-siblings drawn the way they were
  • Let a relative maintain their own branch without touching the rest
  • GEDCOM in and out, so nothing is locked in

Duplicate cleanup

Phone backups run twice. This notices.

Libraries collect copies — a card imported into two places, a folder synced into itself. A cleanup finds them, says which copy it would keep and why, and lets you work through the list at your own pace. Close the browser, come back next week; your decisions are kept.

  • Byte-identical copies and re-saved near-copies, told apart
  • Tags, albums and tagged people move to the copy that survives
  • Everything removed goes to the Recycle Bin, not to oblivion

And

The parts that don't photograph.

Slideshows that become movies

Pick photos, choose a transition, lay your own music under it. Render it to a real 1080p MP4 as a background job — the finished movie files itself back into the gallery. The server suggests slideshows on its own, too: a day with a lot of photos, or everything with one person in it.

Safe to put on the internet

Two-factor sign-in with an authenticator app or emailed codes. Passkeys. Account lockout, IP blocking, trusted home networks, rate limits, a strict Content Security Policy, and email to the admin when something looks wrong. A security page scores how you're doing.

Offline, on the phone

Install it from the browser like an app. Download audiobooks to the device and keep listening on the plane. Progress made offline syncs back when you reconnect; caches are wiped on sign-out, so a shared phone shares nothing.

Yours. Really.

One SQLite database, your files where you put them, backups you can download and restore anywhere. Nightly backup schedule, a Recycle Bin for everything deleted, and a full activity log. Runs in Docker or from the Unraid app store.
